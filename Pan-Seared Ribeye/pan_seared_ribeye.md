# Pan-Seared Ribeye

**Serves 2.** Two boneless ribeyes, fat cap on, 3 cm thick. Ripping-hot cast iron or carbon steel, on the gas. Pan only. Medium-rare is an eye at *54–56 °C* after the rest.

The mushroom cream pepper sauce is a separate recipe. Make it first if they share a plate, and hold it off the boil. Spoon it beside the steak, or under it, once the crust has set. Do not pour it over while the crust is still setting. Do not cook the steak in it.

---

## Ingredients

Weigh each steak. Salt is a rate, taken from that weight. Flaky salt stays at the table.

### In the pan
- Boneless ribeye, fat cap on, 3 cm thick — **2 steaks**. Weigh them
- Kosher salt — **1 g per 100 g** of each steak, on every face, including the fat cap
- Neutral oil — **10 ml**, for the pan

### At the table, not in the pan
- Flaky salt — a pinch, on a slice that tastes shy, after you taste it. If the sauce is on the plate, taste the slice with the sauce first

---

## Method

### 1. Salt the steaks
**Pat** the steaks dry. **Weigh** them. **Salt** each steak at *1 g per 100 g*, on every face, including the fat cap.

The rate is the short brine. Weigh it from the steaks in front of you. Salt each steak from its own weight. Pepper stays off this meat. It is in the sauce.

### 2. Dry the surface
**Leave** them uncovered on a wire rack in the fridge for *1 hour*.

Salted last night, and the face already matte: leave the timer off and go on. A wet shine means the salt is still pulling water, and that face will steam. If you are making the sauce, this hour is when. That work is the other card.

### 3. Read the surface
**Look** at the face of each steak.

> Matte, the salt gone into the meat: they stay in the fridge until the pan is lit.
> Wet and shiny: leave them uncovered. Not yet.

### 4. Heat the pan
**Heat** a seasoned skillet, cast iron or carbon steel, on high gas until it is ripping. **Add** the *10 ml* oil. **Take** the steaks from the fridge and leave them uncovered.

They come out as the pan heats. A long temper warms the eye, and the sear that follows takes it through *56 °C*. The smoke is the tell. At 3 cm the steak stays on this pan. If the sauce is on, it stays off the boil, in its own pan.

### 5. Read the pan
**Look** at the oil.

> It smokes: the fat cap, or the face if there is no real cap.
> No smoke: leave the pan on high. Not yet. Do not lay the steak in.

### 6. Render the fat cap
**Hold** the fat caps on the metal until the edge is browned and fat has rendered into the pan. First look at *45 seconds*.

The eye is still cold, which is why the cap goes now. Both caps only if they fit. The steak that waits gets its cap when it takes the pan. Pale at *45 seconds*: another *20 seconds*, then the face. A steak with no real cap skips this and goes to the face. The oil is already in the pan. Do not add more.

### 7. Sear the first face
**Lay** the steaks down. Both only if bare metal shows between them. If they touch, one at a time, and the other waits on the rack. **Leave** the down face until it releases and is deep brown. First look at *90 seconds*.

Straight from the rack. The cold eye is the time the crust has.

### 8. Read the crust
**Look** at the down face, then **probe** the eye once it is free.

> Deep brown, and it releases: flip. Probe the eye. At *50 °C* already, the second face is color only, then off.
> Blond at *90 seconds*, or still stuck: steaks onto the rack. Heat the pan until the oil smokes. Back on the pale side.
> Black and bitter: that face is done. Probe the eye and pull on the number below. Keep that face off the metal.

### 9. Sear the second face
**Sear** the second face. **Probe** the center of the eye as soon as you flip, and again while it cooks. First look at *60 seconds*.

The spinalis and the fat pocket both read hot. The eye is the steak. If the eye is already *50 °C* at the flip, this face gets color and comes off. No second dose of oil. The rendered fat is the film.

### 10. Pull
**Probe** the eye on each steak. **Pull** at *50 °C*.

> *50–52 °C* in the eye: off the pan, onto a rack, uncovered.
> Under *50 °C*, crust still brown: *20 seconds* more, then probe again.
> *53 °C* or over, or the crust is black: off now.

Off at the first reading of *50 °C*. A *51* or *52* as it leaves the metal is still the pull. The uncovered rest is what carries the eye to *54–56 °C*. The prior for that climb is about *4–6 °C* over the next few minutes. It has not been measured on this pan. The probe at *4 minutes* is the check. The hotter steak comes off first. The other stays until its own eye hits *50 °C*. If they would not fit together, the first rests uncovered while the second sears. Each steak's *4 minutes* starts when that steak leaves the pan. Do not sauce them on the rack.

### 11. Rest
**Rest** uncovered. **Probe** the eye at *4 minutes* from that steak's pull. The landed target is *54–56 °C*.

Rest uncovered, so the crust stays dry. The sauce, if it is on, stays off the boil and off the meat.

### 12. Read the eye
**Probe** the eye. The landed target is *54–56 °C*.

> *54–56 °C*: slice.
> Under *54 °C*: still climbing, another minute, then probe. The climb has stopped: back on the hot pan until the eye reads *53 °C*, then one minute uncovered and probe again. Keep the steak out of the sauce.
> *57 °C* or over: slice now.

### 13. Slice
**Slice** across the grain, in thick pieces. **Leave** the seared faces bare.

Spoon the mushroom cream pepper sauce beside the slices, or under them, once the crust has set. Do not pour it over while the crust is still setting. Do not set the slices in the sauce. Flaky salt goes on a slice that tastes shy, after you taste it. If the sauce is on the plate, taste the slice with the sauce before the pinch.

---

## The call

Pan only, on ripping cast iron or carbon steel. Medium-rare is an eye at *54–56 °C* after the uncovered rest. Pull at *50 °C*. The prior for the climb is about *4–6 °C*, and it has not been measured on this pan, so the probe at *4 minutes* is the cook. The clock is the first look.

At 3 cm an oven start brings the center up, and the sear that follows takes the eye through *56 °C*. The sauce is the other card. Spoon it beside the steak, or under it, once the crust has set. Do not cook the steak in it.

## Red team

**Salt.** 1 g per 100 g, weighed per steak, on every face including the fat cap. That salt stays on the meat. Flaky salt is a pinch after a taste, and only on a shy slice. It is a second salt. The sauce card salts itself. If the sauce is on the plate, taste the bite with it before any flake. Do not salt the sauce from this card. Pepper stays off this meat.

**Acid.** None on the steak. Lemon is the sauce card's finish. A squeeze here stacks with it.

**Fat.** The ribeye is the fat. The pan takes 10 ml of oil once. Rendered cap fat stays in the skillet and is not a second pour. No butter baste. Butter on the steak softens the crust, and the sauce card decides its own mount.

**Heat and doneness.** The load-bearing number is the eye after the rest: 54–56 °C. The pull is 50 °C. A ripping sear on a fridge-cold 3 cm steak leaves a hot outer ring. The prior for the climb, uncovered, over the next few minutes, is about 4–6 °C. That carryover has not been measured on this pan. Pulling at 50 °C aims the band from the low side: a degree past 56 cannot be walked back, and a degree under 54 can go back on the pan for seconds. Under 50 °C stays on the heat. 50–52 °C comes off. 53 °C or over comes off immediately. At 4 minutes from that steak's own pull, 54–56 °C is the serve. Still climbing and under 54 °C waits a minute. Stopped under 54 °C goes back on the hot pan until the eye reads 53 °C, then a minute, then the probe. The second kiss is short, on a steak that has already equalized, so the pull sits closer to the band than the first one did. At 57 °C, slice. Probe the eye. The spinalis and a fat pocket both read high.

The pan has to be smoking before the meat goes down. A hot pan that is not smoking leaves the crust blond at 90 seconds, and minutes on that pan are how the eye arrives before the crust. Cast iron or carbon steel, seasoned, on high gas. Enamel sticks and will not throw the same crust. The 17" paella pan is too wide: the fat runs off, and the eye sees heat the whole time the crust is still pale. One skillet. Both steaks only with bare metal between them. If they touch, one at a time, and the first rests uncovered. Both caps only if they fit. The steak that waits takes its cap when it takes the pan.

At 3 cm the steak stays on the gas the whole way. There is no oven step.

**Wine.** None in this pan.

**Quantity.** Steak weight is the scale, at 3 cm. Salt is a rate. The 10 ml of oil is asserted for a film that smokes, not a measured depth in this skillet. First looks: dry 1 hour, pan heat until the oil smokes, fat cap 45 seconds, first face 90 seconds, second face 60 seconds, rest probe at 4 minutes. None of these were timed on this pan. They are the first look, not the doneness.

## Numbers

**Computed from a rule.** Steak salt is 1 g per 100 g. The rate is the short-brine judgment. The grams are the scale.

**Asserted, not measured in this kitchen.** Oil 10 ml. Pull at 50 °C. Carryover of about 4–6 °C across an uncovered rest, aimed at a landed eye of 54–56 °C. First looks: dry 1 hour, pan heat until the oil smokes, fat cap 45 seconds, first face 90 seconds, second face 60 seconds, rest probe at 4 minutes. None of these were timed on this pan.
