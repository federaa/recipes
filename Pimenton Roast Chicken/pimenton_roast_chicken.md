# Pimentón Roast Chicken, Spiced Jus, Salsa Verde

**Serves 4**, beside other Thanksgiving sides. One chicken of 1.8–2.2 kg. Spatchcock it, salt it, and leave it uncovered for three days. Roast it skin-up in a preheated cast-iron skillet at *210 °C convection*, and paint on brick-red pimentón oil in the last minutes. Serve it with a spiced jus made from a stock started this week, finished with Castelvetrano olives and Savagnin sous voile. Add a hand-chopped salsa verde, and potatoes crushed and crisped in the stock's chicken fat.

## This cook: two birds

The 2026 Thanksgiving cook is **two birds, for 8**. The cook page carries these doses. The card below is written for one bird.

|---|---|---|

| Wings | 1 kg | **1.5 kg**, on two trays |
| Backbones and necks | 1 | **2** |

| Jus finish | 15 ml fat, 1 garlic, 2 g pimentón, 0.5 g cumin, about 15 saffron threads, 50 ml sous voile, 20 g butter, 60 g olives, vinegar up to 5 ml | **× 1.5**: 22 ml, 2, 3 g, 0.75 g, about 20, 75 ml, 30 g, 90 g, up to 8 ml |
| Salsa verde | 30 g parsley, 5 g mint, 15 g capers, 1 anchovy, ½ garlic, 1 zest, 10 ml vinegar, 100 ml oil | **doubled** |
| Potatoes / chicken fat | 800 g / 40 ml | **1.6 kg / 80 ml**, in two pans |

**Why the jus is × 1.5 and not × 2.** The stock is capped by the pot. 1.5 kg of wings, two backbones, and 3.5 L of water come to about 5.9 L in the 8 L Staub, which still holds a bare simmer with the lid ajar. 2 kg of wings would crowd it. 450 ml of jus base is about 56 ml a plate for eight. That is still a pour, but not Madre's brothy 75 ml. For the full 75 ml a plate, make a second pot of stock.

**The oven.** Two 30 cm skillets with handles probably will not sit side by side on one Miele shelf. Set them in the cold oven on Monday to check. On two shelves, swap them top to bottom at the first probe, because the fan does not even out two levels by itself. From there each bird has its own probe and its own pull. Pull each one at 63 °C breast and 80 °C thigh, without waiting for the other.

**Not cooked at this scale, or at the other.** The two-bird numbers are the one-bird card scaled, and they are asserted.

## Where it comes from

This is in the register of Madre, Greenpoint. Their chicken is a half bird with crisp skin "layered with different Spanish spices", a spiced jus poured over it, and salsa verde on top (The Modest Menu, July 2024). An earlier review put it beside confit potatoes, escarole, olives and black garlic. Nothing published says how they cook it. This is not their recipe.

Taken from Madre: the spices on the skin, the jus, the salsa verde, the potatoes and the olives. Left out: the escarole, because the table has sides, and the black garlic, because it would be a second sweet-dark note fighting the pimentón.

## Ingredients

### The bird · Monday
- Whole chicken — **1.8–2.2 kg**
- Fine sea salt — **8 g per kg** of the spatchcocked weight (0.8%)
- Black pepper — **2 g**

### Stock · Tuesday · Staub oval
- Chicken wings — **1 kg**
- The backbone and neck from the spatchcock
- Onion, quartered — **150 g**
- Carrot, in chunks — **100 g**
- Garlic — **½ head**, halved across
- Thyme — **3 sprigs**
- Bay leaf — **1**
- Black peppercorns — **2 g**
- Cold water — **2.5 L**, to cover
- No salt

### Pimentón oil · Thursday
- Olive oil — **30 ml**
- Smoked pimentón, sweet (dulce) — **3 g**
- Smoked pimentón, hot (picante) — **1 g**
- Garlic, grated — **1 small clove**
- Thyme leaves — **1 g**

### Spiced jus · Thursday
- Jus base from Wednesday — about **300 ml**
- Chicken fat from the stock — **15 ml**
- Garlic, thinly sliced — **1 clove**
- Smoked pimentón, sweet — **2 g**
- Cumin, ground — **0.5 g**
- Saffron — **about 15 threads**, steeped in **15 ml** hot water
- Tissot Savagnin Sous Voile 2020 — **50 ml**

- Castelvetrano olives, pitted and halved — **60 g**
- Sherry vinegar — **up to 5 ml**, only after tasting with the salsa verde

### Potatoes · Thursday

## Method

### Monday

#### 1. Spatchcock and weigh
**Cut** out the backbone with shears and keep it, with the neck, for the stock. **Press** the breastbone flat. **Tuck** the wing tips behind the breast. **Weigh** the bird.

**Set** it in your largest cast-iron skillet. It should lie flat, with the legs inside the rim. That skillet is Thursday's roasting pan, preheated. If the bird is over 2.2 kg, or the legs ride up the wall, it roasts on a rack over a roasting tray instead, and the drippings come from the tray.

#### 2. Salt and air-dry
**Salt** at *8 g per kg* of the spatchcocked weight. Put about a third under the skin of the breast and thighs, and the rest over every surface. **Pepper** it with *2 g*. **Leave** it skin-up on a rack, uncovered, on the bottom shelf of the fridge until Thursday.

The skin should go tight, matte, and a little translucent. That dry skin is the crisp. Do not rinse it, and do not oil it before the roast.

### Tuesday

#### 3. Roast the bones
**Roast** *1 kg* wings, the backbone, and the neck on a roasting tray at *200 °C convection* until deep brown, turning them once. First look at *45 minutes*.

#### 4. Deglaze and cover
**Move** the bones to the Staub. **Pour** *200 ml* water onto the hot tray, scrape up the fond, and add that to the pot. **Add** the onion, carrot, garlic, thyme, bay, peppercorns, and *2.5 L* cold water.

#### 5. Simmer the stock
**Bring** it up slowly over medium-low and skim the foam. **Hold** it at a bare simmer, an occasional bubble, with the lid ajar, for *4 hours*.

A boil emulsifies the fat into the stock and clouds the jus. No salt, because the stock gets reduced by five.

#### 6. Strain and chill
**Strain** it through a fine sieve, pressing lightly. **Chill** it overnight. Expect about *1.5 L*.

### Wednesday

#### 7. Lift the fat
**Lift** the set fat off the top and keep it. Thursday wants *15 ml* for the jus and *40 ml* for the potatoes.

#### 8. Reduce the jus base
**Reduce** the stock at a steady simmer in a wide enameled braiser to about *300 ml*. First look at *45 minutes*.

> It coats a spoon, and a finger line across the back holds: chill it. It sets firm cold.
> It is thin and runs back: keep reducing. Volume is the prior, the spoon decides.
> It is gluey, or catching at the edges: off the heat, and loosen it with water, *30 ml* at a time.

### Thursday

#### 9. Boil the potatoes · S − 2:00
**Start** *800 g* potatoes in cold water salted at *10 g per litre*. **Simmer** them until a knife slides through, about *20 minutes*. **Drain** them and let them steam dry for *5 minutes*. **Crush** each one flat to about *1.5 cm* and hold them on a tray.

#### 10. Make the salsa verde · S − 1:40
**Chop** *30 g* parsley and *5 g* mint by knife. **Mince** *15 g* capers, *1* anchovy, and *½* garlic clove together. **Stir** in the lemon zest, *10 ml* sherry vinegar, and *100 ml* olive oil.

Use a knife, not a blender: a blender bruises the herbs to grey and emulsifies the oil. Do not salt it. The capers and the anchovy are its salt, and it gets tasted with the chicken.

#### 11. Make the pimentón oil
**Warm** *30 ml* olive oil with the grated garlic and *1 g* thyme for *1 minute*, just until it smells sweet. **Take** it off the heat, then **stir** in *3 g* sweet and *1 g* hot pimentón.

Off the heat, because pimentón scorches in seconds in hot oil and turns bitter.

#### 12. Heat the oven and the skillet · S − 1:30
**Heat** the oven to *210 °C convection* with the cast-iron skillet inside, for *20 minutes*. **Take** the bird out of the fridge, uncovered on its rack.

Twenty minutes on the counter is the floor; pulling it up to *S − 2:00* (when you start the potatoes) knocks more chill off the bone side. Keep it uncovered so the dry skin does not sweat, and do not leave it out past an hour.

#### 13. Roast · S − 1:10
**Lay** the bird skin-up in the hot skillet. **Roast** it on the middle shelf. First probe at *30 minutes*, in the deepest part of the breast.

#### 14. Read the breast

> At about *55 °C*: paint it now.
> Under *50 °C*: *5 more minutes*, then probe again.
> Skin already mahogany at the edges: paint it now, and probe every *3 minutes* from here.

#### 15. Paint and finish
**Brush** the pimentón oil over all the skin. **Roast** until the breast reads *63 °C* and the thigh *80 °C*. First look at *8 minutes*.

The oil goes on late because pimentón burns long before the bird is done. At 210 °C it has minutes, not the whole roast.

#### 16. Pull

> Breast *63 °C*, thigh *80 °C* or over: lift the bird to a rack to rest.
> Breast under *63 °C*: *3 more minutes*, then probe again.
> Breast *63 °C* but thigh under *78 °C*: cut the legs off at the hip, put them back in the skillet skin-up for *5–8 minutes*, and rest the breast.

#### 17. Rest, uncovered · S − 0:30
**Rest** it uncovered on the rack for *15 minutes*. **Pour** the skillet drippings into a cup and let the fat rise.

Uncovered, so the skin stays crisp. A tent steams it. The breast carries over to about *65–66 °C*.

#### 18. Crisp the potatoes
**Heat** *40 ml* chicken fat in a second cast-iron or carbon-steel pan over medium-high. **Crisp** the crushed potatoes until deep gold on both sides, about *6 minutes* a side.

#### 19. Build the jus
**Warm** *15 ml* chicken fat with the sliced garlic over medium until the garlic is pale gold. **Take** it off the heat, stir in *2 g* pimentón and *0.5 g* cumin for *20 seconds*, then **pour** in *50 ml* sous voile and the saffron with its water. **Reduce** it by half. **Add** the jus base and the drippings from under the fat, and simmer until it coats a spoon.

#### 20. Mount and finish the jus
**Whisk** in *20 g* cold butter off the boil. **Add** *60 g* olives to warm through.

The wine goes in at the finish, not into the stock on Tuesday, because four hours of simmering would take its nose. This way its flor note reaches the plate.

#### 21. Taste the jus
**Taste** it on a piece of chicken with a little salsa verde.

> It snaps into focus: done.
> Flat: *5 ml* sherry vinegar, then salt in *0.5 g* steps, tasting between each.
> Salty: no salt. Loosen it with water, *30 ml* at a time.

Taste it with the salsa verde, because that is how it is eaten. The salsa verde already brings vinegar and zest, so the jus vinegar comes second and only if it is needed.

#### 22. Carve · S − 0:10
**Cut** off the legs and split each at the knee joint. **Take** the breasts off the bone in whole lobes, then **slice** them thick across. Keep the skin up throughout.

#### 23. Plate
**Pool** the jus and olives on a warm platter. **Set** the chicken on it, skin up and clear of the liquid. **Spoon** salsa verde across the cut faces, not over the skin. **Pile** the potatoes alongside and finish them with flaky salt. **Pass** the rest of the jus at the table.

Madre pours its jus over the bird. Here it goes under, because a jus poured over skin you dried for three days softens it before it reaches the table.

## The call

Roast it spatchcocked, salted three days and uncovered, skin-up in preheated cast iron at *210 °C convection*. Pull at a breast of *63 °C* and a thigh of *80 °C*, and rest it uncovered. Paint on the pimentón in the last minutes. Make the jus from a stock started this week, and finish it with Savagnin, olives, butter, and salt only after tasting it with the salsa verde.

## Red team

**Temperature and vessel.** High-heat spatchcock is usually written for about 230 °C conventional. Lowered 20 °C for the Miele, that is *210 °C convection*. That figure is converted, not cooked here. The skillet is preheated so the bone side starts cooking immediately and the bird finishes evenly. The 17" paella pan was the first thought, but at 43 cm, plus handles, it very likely overhangs the Miele cavity, so measure it before using it. Cast iron is fine for a dry roast. The jus is made in the enameled braiser and an enameled saucepan, because it is salty and wet. The drippings come out of the cast iron, and the skillet is not deglazed with wine.

**Volatile flavors.** Pimentón scorches, so it goes on late on the skin and off the heat in the jus. The sous voile goes into the jus at the finish, not the stock. Saffron is steeped and added at the finish. The herbs in the salsa verde are cut the same afternoon.

**Fat.** There is a lot of it: the skin, the chicken fat on the potatoes, 20 g of butter in the jus, and 100 ml of oil in the salsa verde. The salsa verde's vinegar and lemon zest are the counterweight, which is the lesson from the morel cream. The stock is skimmed and its fat lifted cold, so the jus is not greasy.

**Salt, stacked.**

| Source | Salt | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Bird, 0.8% of about 2 kg spatchcocked | about 16 g | Computed. Bones are about a quarter of the weight, so the meat sees about 1% |
| Stock and jus base | none added | The reduction concentrates only what the bones gave |
| Olives, 60 g | about 2 g | Asserted, brined olives at about 3–4% |
| Capers 15 g, rinsed, and 1 anchovy | about 1–1.5 g | Asserted |
| Potato water, 10 g per litre | about 0.5% in the potato | Asserted, from typical pickup |
| Butter | none | Unsalted |

The bird is set at 0.8% instead of the house 1% because the plate brings olives, capers, and anchovy beside it. The jus gate tastes everything together and salts last, in half-gram steps.

**Acid, stacked.** There is the salsa verde's 10 ml of sherry vinegar plus lemon zest, the 50 ml of sous voile reduced by half, the olive brine, and an optional 5 ml of vinegar in the jus. That last one is conditional, and it is tasted with the salsa verde first, so the two vinegars do not double up.

**Technique gaps, closed.**
- The bird might not lie flat in the skillet. That is checked on Monday, with a rack and tray as the fallback.
- Breast and thigh might finish apart. The pull gate takes the legs back in.
- A tent would soften the skin, so it rests uncovered.
- A boiled stock would cloud the jus, so it is held at a bare simmer.
- Reducing to a number can overshoot, so the spoon test decides.
- Pimentón burns on the skin, so it is painted late, with a gate on the skin color.

**Technique stacking.** This is a new build. The bourguignon rule says departures from a tested recipe should be few. There is no tested recipe here, so the rule becomes: no component may be novel. Each one is the standard version of its technique, and the inventions are limited to the spice and wine choices, which are flavor, not structure. If something has to go on the day, the salsa verde and the potatoes can be dropped without touching the bird or the jus.

**Quantity.** A 2 kg bird is four plates with other sides. 300 ml of jus is about 75 ml a plate, a brothy plate the way Madre does it. 800 g of potatoes serves four. For eight, see *This cook: two birds* above. The stock is capped by the 8 L pot at about 450 ml of jus base.

**Wine.** Use the **2020 Tissot Savagnin Sous Voile**, of which the export lists 7 bottles. 50 ml goes in the jus and the rest is poured at the table. It is the Jura's flor wine, the closest thing in the cellar to the fino a Spanish kitchen would reach for, and it meets the pimentón, saffron, and olives head-on. For a red, pour the **2022 Tissot Trousseau Singulier** (4 bottles), which is light enough not to flatten the chicken.

## Numbers

**Computed.** Bird salt is 8 g per kg, at 0.8%. The oven is 210 °C convection, from about 230 °C conventional minus 20 °C.

**Asserted, not cooked in this kitchen.** Everything else:
- The roast's first probe at 30 minutes, and roughly 40–50 minutes in total.
- The pull at a breast of 63 °C and a thigh of 80 °C. The 63 °C relies on carryover through the uncovered rest to reach about 65–66 °C.
- The bone roast at 45 minutes, the stock at 4 hours and about 1.5 L, and the reduction to about 300 ml in about 45 minutes.
- All of the spice, oil, herb, wine, and olive quantities.
- The potato timings.
- The salt carried by the olives, capers, anchovy, and potato water.
