# Miso-Braised Chicken Legs (Barley-Miso, Seafood Stock)

Bone-in, skin-on chicken legs seared in seasoned metal, then partial-braised in enamel, built on a three-miso
system. As-cooked version: Korean seafood instant stock in place of dashi, soy cut, full
finishing miso committed. Two vessels: seasoned metal for the sear, enamel or ceramic for the braise.

**Serves:** 3 (3 whole legs) · **Total time:** ~90 min, mostly hands-off

---

## The three-miso logic

Each miso does one non-overlapping job:

- **Awase (barley) miso** — earthy, nutty, robust. Goes into the *braise* for background depth.
  This is the miso-ni backbone.
- **Juwari kōji miso** — high-kōji rice miso, strong glutamate. Goes into the *finish* for umami
  and body.
- **White sweet miso** — sweetest, mildest, palest. Goes into the *finish* for sweetness balance
  (and can lacquer the skin if you glaze).

Miso is added in two stages because prolonged high heat drives off its volatile aromatics — a
modest amount in the braise for depth, the brighter misos whisked in at the end, off the boil, to
restore the fresh fermented top note.

---

## Ingredients

### Chicken
- Bone-in, skin-on chicken legs — **3 legs** (about **1.0–1.2 kg**)
- Kosher salt — **a pinch** (no weighed dose; the stock and the miso carry the salt)
- Neutral oil — **a thin film**, for the sear pan

### Braise base
- Korean seafood instant stock — **3.5 g** sachet in **400 ml** hot water
- Sake — **80 ml**
- Mirin — **40 ml**
- Awase (barley) miso — **25 g**
- Ginger, sliced — **20 g**
- Garlic, smashed — **3 cloves**
- Scallion whites — **3 scallions** (greens saved to finish)
- Dried chili — **1 chili** (optional)

> **No soy.** The seafood stock is a seasoned base (복합조미식품) and carries its own salt — soy
> would push it over. If you sub in pure kombu-katsuo dashi instead, add back **10 ml soy**.

### Finish (off the boil)
- Juwari kōji miso — **30 g**
- White sweet miso — **15 g**
- Cold butter — **8 g**
- Rice vinegar or yuzu — a few drops

### To serve
- Scallion greens — from the **3 scallions**
- Toasted sesame — **to taste**
- Shichimi — **to taste**

## Method

1. **Prep.** Pat legs bone-dry, salt *lightly* only (the two-stage miso + seasoned stock bring
   most of the salt; a braise concentrates it). Reconstitute the stock and taste it — savory,
   lightly saline. Oven to **160 °C / 325 °F**.

2. **Render in seasoned metal, not the braising vessel.** Use a seasoned cast-iron or carbon-steel
   pan for the sear — its seasoning is a release layer that ceramic and enamel don't have, so the
   skin lets go clean instead of tearing. Preheat the pan properly, add a thin film of neutral oil
   until it shimmers, *then* lay the legs skin-down. Don't dry-start on ceramic/enamel and don't
   render in the braiser — both grip lean skin and tear it. Medium-high, and don't move the legs
   until the skin releases on its own and reads deep gold (a nudge with tongs tells you: glued =
   wait, slides = go). Flip ~1 min to set the flesh side, then transfer all legs to a plate.

   > **Why split pans:** the sear wants a release surface (seasoned metal); the braise wants an inert
   > one (enamel/ceramic) so the salty, acidic liquid doesn't pick up metallic notes over 45–60 min.
   > Each pan does the job it's good at. The render's fond stays in the metal pan — deglaze it into
   > the base below so none of that flavor is lost.

3. **Base.** In the enameled/ceramic braising vessel, warm a thin film of the rendered fat (spooned
   from the sear pan). Sweat aromatics ~1 min, fragrant, no browning on the garlic. Add sake, then
   pour it into the *sear pan* too and scrape up that fond, returning it all to the braiser — reduce
   by half (~1–2 min) to cook off the alcohol.
   Add stock + mirin, whisk in the **25 g awase miso** until dissolved. Bring to a *bare* simmer.

   > **Fond vs. scorch check:** if bits stick and darken during the render, deglaze with a splash
   > of sake off-heat first. Brown bits that dissolve = flavor, keep them. Hard black flecks that
   > won't dissolve = scorched, strain them out before building the base — bitterness telegraphs
   > loudly in a delicate miso braise.

4. **Braise.** Legs back in skin-up, liquid to **mid-thigh with the skin proud** of the surface.
   Lid on, into the oven **45–60 min**, to **82–85 °C** in the thickest part of the thigh. Chase
   the probe, not the clock — dark meat wants that collagen conversion, not the 74 °C minimum.

5. **Salt gate (critical).** Pull the legs to rest, tented. Put the pot on low; reduce a couple
   minutes only if the liquid looks thin. **Taste the liquid before any finishing miso.** With the
   seasoned stock and no soy, this reading decides everything:
   - Savory/balanced → commit the full finishing slurry.
   - Assertively salty → start with half (~20 g), taste, add more only for body.

6. **Finish off the boil.** Heat off or barest tremble — **do not boil the finishing miso** or you
   flatten the kōji/white aromatics. Whisk in the slurry (kōji + white, loosened with a ladle of
   hot liquid), mount the cold butter for gloss, finish with a few drops of vinegar/yuzu for lift.
   Taste and stop the moment it snaps into focus.

7. **Plate.** Optional broiler re-crisp, legs skin-up 2–3 min. **Nap the sauce *under* the legs**,
   not over, so the skin stays dry on top. Scallion greens, sesame, shichimi.

## Notes & what actually happened this cook

- **Stock swap:** built on Korean seafood instant stock (blue crab / shrimp / anchovy / kelp /
  oyster). The anchovy-kelp backbone reads as a Korean niboshi-kombu dashi; the crab/shrimp/oyster
  layer adds marine umami. Pushes the dish a half-step toward a Japanese-Korean hybrid — coherent
  with miso, not a derail. There's also a **spicy coin-type stock (칼칼한 맛)** on hand — subbing
  that turns this into a spicy Korean-Japanese braise and would want the aromatics re-tuned.
- **Salt math:** cutting the soy was the right move against the seasoned stock. The salt gate at
  step 5 read savory-with-a-little-chicken-fat, so the **full 45 g finishing slurry** went in with
  no holdback. The acid at the end is what keeps "savory + fat" from reading heavy — don't skip it.
- **Two pans, by surface:** sear in seasoned cast iron / carbon steel, braise in enamel or ceramic.
  This was a hard-won correction — rendering skin-on legs directly in a ceramic or enameled vessel
  tears the skin and leaves residue, because those surfaces have no seasoning to act as a release
  layer; the skin grips before its own fat can lubricate it. Seasoned metal releases clean. The
  braise still wants the inert enamel/ceramic so the salty, acidic liquid stays free of metallic
  notes over 45–60 min. So the vessels split by job — release surface for the sear, inert surface
  for the braise — and the sear-pan fond gets deglazed into the base so nothing is lost.
- **The render doesn't need perfection.** The skin gets a braise and an optional broiler re-crisp
  afterward, so final skin quality is decided at the end, not at the sear. Minor tearing there is
  cosmetic — don't chase a flawless lift at a step whose output gets rewritten.
- **Metric is authoritative** throughout; no conversions applied.

## Arithmetic — how the numbers are derived

Three kinds of number in this recipe, and they should not be trusted equally:

**Stock reconstitution (asserted, to-taste).** Seafood sachet is 3.5 g. Used at 3.5 g : 400 ml ≈
0.9% solids. The sachet's own dosing is ~1 per 350 ml, so 400 ml runs it slightly lean — that's
deliberate (headroom for the miso), but taste and concentrate/dilute to your palate; this ratio is
asserted, not measured.

**Salt percentages (estimates — flagged).** The ~6% (white) / ~9% (kōji) / ~8% (awase) figures are
category ballparks, not label readings. They justify cutting the soy and leaning on the salt gate;
they are *not* precise enough to compute a dose from. The salt gate at step 5 is the real
instrument — taste the reduced liquid and dose finishing miso to it.

**Base quantities (3 legs, ~1.0–1.2 kg):** stock 400 ml · sake 80 ml · mirin 40 ml · awase 25 g (braise) ·
ginger 20 g · garlic 3 cloves · scallions 3 · chili 1 · finish kōji 30 g + white 15 g · butter 8 g.

### Scaling to 6 legs (2× the protein)

The multiplier is *not* uniform, because different quantities are governed by different things:

- **Liquid — geometry-driven, ×1.5 as a starting pour.** Target is liquid to mid-thigh with skin
  proud; in a wider vessel the footprint grows sublinearly, so liquid scales below the protein
  factor. Start at 1.5× and *top up to the line* — the line is the real spec, 1.5× is just where to
  begin. → stock 400→**600**, sake 80→**120**, mirin 40→**60**.
- **Braise miso — tracks the liquid, not the meat.** It seasons the braising liquid, so it scales by
  the liquid factor: 25 × (600 / 400) = 37.5 → **40 g** (rounded).
- **Aromatics — linear with the meat, ×2.** ginger 20 g→**40 g**, garlic 3 cloves→**6 cloves**, scallions 3→**6**,
  chili 1→**2**.
- **Finishing miso — a ceiling, dosed to taste.** It seasons the final sauce, whose volume tracks
  the liquid (×1.5): 45 × 1.5 ≈ 67 g → print **kōji 45 + white 23** as the *ceiling*. The grocery
  list rounds this up (buy 50 + 25) for shopping buffer; the actual dose is whatever the salt gate
  allows, and is usually less.
- **Butter — gloss, non-critical, ~×1.5–2.** 8 g→**15 g**. Not a number worth defending.

Net: liquid and its dependent (braise miso) at ~1.5×; everything tied to the meat at 2×; the finish
to taste. Any single "2× everything" would over-flood the pan and over-salt the sauce.

## Next-time levers
- **Lid-on vs lid-off:** lid-on (as cooked) gives silkier meat and protects the barley miso from
  going harsh via over-reduction. Lid-off exposes more skin and reduces harder — viable given the
  Le Creuset's high heat ceiling, if you want more skin character.
- **Glaze route:** brush a thin white-miso + mirin wash before the broiler for a lacquered skin —
  watch it constantly, miso sugar scorches fast.
- **North African cross-build:** harissa + barley-miso braise converge well on the earthy-umami
  axis if you want to push this out of the Japanese-clean register.
