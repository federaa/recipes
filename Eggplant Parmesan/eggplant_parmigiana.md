# Melanzane alla Parmigiana — Roasted Build

**Serves 4–6 · ~2.5 L baking dish · Miele convection + one wide pot for sugo**

A Neapolitan-style parmigiana: **roasted, not breaded**. Roasting (vs. the
Italian-American fry-and-bread cutlet) is the deliberate choice here — it avoids
turning the eggplant into an oil sponge, concentrates flavour through Maillard,
runs mostly hands-off in the convection oven, and gives a cleaner, better-setting
layer structure that holds a slice. The whole dish lives or dies on **moisture
control**, so the method is organised around that.

---

## Ingredients

### Eggplant
- Globe eggplant — **1.3 kg** (≈ 3 medium)
- Fine salt, for purging — **~15 g**
- Olive oil, for roasting — **60–80 ml** (brushed, not pooled)

### Tomato sugo
- Whole peeled San Marzano (or good plum tomatoes) — **800 g** (2 tins)
- Olive oil — **40 ml**
- Garlic, thinly sliced — **3 cloves**
- Basil stems — from the one bunch, into the sugo

### Cheese and layers
- Low-moisture mozzarella, *or* well-drained fior di latte — **350 g**
- Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated — **100 g**
- Basil leaves — **~20**, from that same bunch, for the layers and the plate

### Leave in the cupboard until a step names it
- Passata — **200 g**, only if the crushed tomatoes stay chunky and you want the sugo smoother
- Salt for the sugo — **4–6 g**. A light pinch of this goes in with the tomatoes. The rest only if the taste is shy
- Sugar — a pinch, only if the tin reads sharp
- Chili flakes — a pinch, only if you want heat in the sugo
- Pecorino Romano — **20 g**, only if you want it with the parm

---

## Method

### 1. Purge the eggplant
Slice into **10 mm rounds**. Salt both sides, rack 45–60 min until beads of amber
liquid sit on the surface, then **press and pat bone-dry**.

*Why:* draws free water so the slices **roast instead of steam** and drink far
less oil; lightly seasons; marginally reduces bitterness. Modern globe eggplant is
bred low-bitter, so treat the purge as a **moisture and oil-absorption control**,
not a bitterness ritual.

### 2. Roast — the doneness gate
**220 °C convection.** Brush both sides with oil — *brush, don't pool*; even purged
eggplant will drink a puddle. Single layer, parchment or racks. **20–25 min,
flipping once**, to **deep gold, fully collapsed and creamy** at the centre with
caramelised edges.

Undercooked eggplant is the **most common structural failure**: pale and rubbery
slices never meld and read leathery in the finished bake. Take them fully soft.
- **Pale / springy** → not done. Keep going.
- **Deep gold / slumped / creamy** → right. Pull.
- **Charred, acrid** → gone bitter. Pull immediately; the char telegraphs.

### 3. Build the sugo — the trail has to hold
Warm the **40 ml** oil. If you are using the pinch of chili flakes, they go in now.
Infuse the sliced garlic to **pale gold only** (any browning = bitterness).
Hand-crush the tomatoes. Add a light pinch from the **4–6 g** sugo salt, and the
basil stems. If the crush stays chunky and you want it smoother, stir in the
**200 g** passata. Simmer **25–35 min**. Draw a spoon through the sauce on the
bottom of the pan. The line it leaves stays clear, and no watery pool floods back.

*Why:* this is the single biggest lever against soupy parmigiana. Pull the garlic
if you want a cleaner sauce; discard the basil stems.

### 4. Cheese prep + the salt gate
If using **fresh fior di latte**: slice or tear and **drain on towels 30+ min**,
then pat — fresh mozzarella can shed 20 %+ of its weight in water straight into
your bake. Low-moisture mozzarella skips this. Grate the parm.

**Taste the sugo.** The **4–6 g** is the whole sugo salt, and the pinch already
came out of it. Purge salt, the tinned tomatoes, and the parm are stacking, and
the pecorino too if you are using the **20 g**.
- **Shy** → a pinch more from that same 4–6 g. Taste again. Stop while it is still a little under.
- **Just under, or already seasoned** → add no more salt. The cheese carries the rest.

If it still reads sharp once the salt is right, a pinch of sugar.

### 5. Assemble
Thin film of sauce on the base (anti-stick). Then per layer:
**eggplant (overlapping, no gaps) → sauce (spooned, not drowned) → torn mozzarella
(restrained) → parm → torn basil.** Build **3 layers**.

Finish the top with **sauce + a heavy hand of parm, and only a little mozzarella** —
surface mozzarella goes leathery and oily, while parm browns beautifully. If you
are using the **20 g** pecorino, it goes with the parm.

### 6. Bake
**190 °C convection, 30–35 min**, until the edges bubble and the top is burnished
(internal ~75 °C if you want a number). If the top colours too fast, tent with foil.

### 7. The rest — the structural gate
**Rest 25–30 min minimum** before cutting. Out of the oven the layers are molten
and a hot cut slides into soup. Better still: **cool completely and reheat** —
parmigiana is famously better the next day, fully set.
- **Test slice holds its wall** → ready.
- **Collapses / runs** → give it another 15 min.

### 8. Serve
A few of the basil leaves, a thread of good oil, a last dusting of parm.

## Red-team — failure modes

- **Soupy parm** — the headline failure. **Three water sources**: under-reduced
  sauce, wet mozzarella, under-rested bake. Control all three, not one.
- **Greasy** — too much roasting oil pooled, or too much surface mozzarella. Brush
  the oil; keep mozzarella restrained and mostly *internal*.
- **Bland / flat** — under-seasoned sugo (salt-shy) plus under-roasted eggplant (no
  Maillard). Season the sauce; brown the eggplant.
- **Leathery top** — too much mozzarella on the surface. Finish with parm.
- **Bitter** — browned garlic or charred eggplant. Infuse garlic to pale gold; pull
  eggplant before char.

## Substitution logic
- **No fior di latte** → low-moisture mozzarella (arguably *better* here — less
  water to manage).
- **No San Marzano** → any good **whole plum**, hand-crushed. Avoid pre-diced tins;
  the added calcium chloride keeps them from breaking down into sauce.
- **Breaded variation (Italian-American)** → dredge slices flour → egg →
  breadcrumb + parm, shallow-fry, then layer. Crisper and richer, but heavier and
  greasier — roasting is the cleaner build.
- **Grill variation** → char slices on the plancha or grill for a smoke note.

## Wine
Tomato acidity + savoury cheese wants a **medium-bodied, high-acid, savoury-fruited
Italian red** — Chianti Classico (Sangiovese), Aglianico, or Barbera — over
anything big and tannic, in line with the savoury-fruited-over-tannin principle.
