Morel Mushroom Pappardelle
with Cream & Parmigiano-Reggiano

A triple-reduction pasta — wine, stock, and cream built in sequence — finished with a cold butter mount and lemon acid to balance the richness. The morel is the entire point.

Serves4
Active time~35 minutes
VesselWide stainless or carbon steel skillet

Ingredients

  • Fresh morel mushrooms350 g
  • Fresh pappardelle400 g
  • Shallots, brunoise-cut3
  • Unsalted butter, cold & cubed, divided80 g
  • Dry white wine or Madeira120 ml
  • Mushroom or chicken stock, warm200 ml
  • Heavy cream, 36%+ fat300 ml
  • Parmigiano-Reggiano, microplaned80 g
  • Fresh thyme sprigs2
  • Fresh tarragon, finely chopped5 g
  • White pepper, freshly ground1 g
  • Freshly grated nutmeg1 pinch
  • Lemon, juice & zest½
  • Finishing extra-virgin olive oil15 ml
  • Kosher saltto taste

Method

  1. 1

    Clean the morels

    Do not wash under running water — they will waterlog and steam instead of sear. Use a dry pastry brush to remove visible dirt. Halve each one lengthwise and inspect the hollow interior for grit or insects. If heavy grit is present, a very quick dunk in cold salted water is acceptable, but pat completely dry before the pan.

  2. 2

    Salt your pasta water

    Bring at least 5L of water to a full rolling boil. Season aggressively — roughly 10g salt per litre, until it tastes like the sea. Keep lid on to maintain heat.

  3. 3

    Sear the morels

    Get your skillet genuinely ripping hot before anything goes in. Add 30g of the butter — the pan should be hot enough that it barely has time to foam. Add morels in a single layer; work in two batches if needed. Cook undisturbed 2–3 minutes until deeply caramelised on one side. Toss and cook 1–2 minutes more. Season with salt and a little of the 1 g white pepper. Transfer to a plate. Do not wipe the pan.

    ⏱ 4 minutes
  4. 4

    Build the shallot base

    Reduce heat to medium. Add 30g butter to the same pan, letting it lift the fond. Once melted, add shallots and cook gently 4–5 minutes until completely softened, sweet, and translucent — no colour at all.

    ⏱ 5 minutes
  5. 5

    Deglaze and reduce the wine

    Increase to high heat. Pour in the wine and scrape the pan immediately. Boil hard until almost completely evaporated and shallots look glazed — about 2–3 minutes. All raw alcohol should be gone; trust your nose.

    ⏱ 3 minutes
  6. 6

    Reduce the stock

    Pour in the stock. Keep heat high and reduce by half — about 3–4 minutes. You should have roughly 100ml of intense, fragrant liquid. This concentrates savoury depth before cream enters the pan.

    ⏱ 3–4 minutes
  7. 7

    Cream reduction to nappe

    Add cream and tuck in thyme sprigs. Bring to a lively simmer — not a rolling boil. Reduce by one-third, stirring occasionally, 6–8 minutes until the sauce coats a spoon and a finger dragged through it leaves a clean line. Remove thyme. Return seared morels. Season with salt, the rest of the 1 g white pepper, and the pinch of nutmeg. Hold at a gentle simmer.

    ⏱ 7 minutes
  8. 8

    Cook the pasta

    Drop pappardelle into boiling salted water. Fresh pasta cooks in 2–3 minutes — pull it just before al dente. Before draining, ladle out at least 250ml pasta cooking water and set aside.

  9. 9

    Marry pasta and sauce

    Transfer pasta directly from pot to sauce pan with tongs — bring the clinging water with it. Set over medium heat. Add 45–60ml pasta water. Toss vigorously and continuously for 60–90 seconds until the sauce is glossy, cohesive, and clings to every strand. Add pasta water a splash at a time if it looks tight.

    ⏱ 90 seconds
  10. 10

    Mount with butter and Parmesan

    Pull the pan off the heat entirely. Add the remaining 20g cold cubed butter first, tossing to melt — this is the gloss step. Then add Parmigiano-Reggiano in two additions, tossing constantly. If the sauce tightens, a splash of pasta water loosens it.

  11. 11

    Acid and plate

    Squeeze in the lemon juice and grate in a few strokes of zest. Toss once more — this is what makes the morel flavour lift and the richness feel clean. Taste and adjust salt. Warm bowls 60 seconds with hot water, then dry. Twirl portions into each bowl. Spoon remaining morels and sauce over top. Scatter tarragon and finish with olive oil. Serve within 90 seconds.

Notes & Variations

Searing temperature

The pan must be ripping hot before butter goes in. A warm pan causes fresh morels to steam — you'll never recover the crust.

Batch searing

If morels are large, sear in two batches without exception. One crowded batch of steamed mushrooms will flatten the entire dish.

Stock quality

You're reducing this by half, so it gets amplified. Homemade or a quality store-bought — not a bouillon cube.

Madeira upgrade

Swap white wine for Madeira or dry Marsala. Adds a nutty, oxidative depth that pairs extraordinarily well with fresh morels.

The acid is structural

Lemon at the finish cuts the cream, brightens morel flavour, and separates a restaurant finish from a heavy sauce. Non-negotiable.

Fresh pasta

250g 00 flour, 4 yolks + 1 whole egg, 5ml olive oil, pinch salt. Knead 10 min, rest 30 min, roll to 2mm. Cut wide pappardelle.

Emulsification rescue

If the sauce looks broken or greasy at the finish, add a splash of pasta water off the heat and toss hard — it re-binds immediately.

Storage

Sauce alone keeps 3 days refrigerated. Reheat gently with a splash of cream. Never microwave.
