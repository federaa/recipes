# Morel Mushroom Pappardelle with Cream & Parmigiano-Reggiano

**Serves:** 4  
**Active time:** ~35 minutes

A triple-reduction French-Italian pasta — wine, stock, and cream built in sequence — finished with a cold butter mount and lemon acid to balance the richness. The morel is the entire point; every other decision serves it.

---

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Amount |
|---|---|
| Fresh morel mushrooms | 350g |
| Fresh pappardelle | 400g |
| Shallots, brunoise-cut | 3 |
| Unsalted butter, cold and cubed, divided | 80g |
| Dry white wine (or Madeira) | 120ml |
| Mushroom or chicken stock, warm | 200ml |
| Heavy cream, 36%+ fat | 300ml |
| Parmigiano-Reggiano, freshly microplaned | 80g |
| Fresh thyme sprigs | 2 |
| Fresh tarragon leaves, finely chopped | 5g |
| White pepper, freshly ground | 1g |
| Freshly grated nutmeg | 1 pinch |
| Lemon (juice and zest) | ½ |
| Finishing extra-virgin olive oil | 15ml |
| Kosher salt | to taste |

---

## Method

### 1. Clean the morels
Do not wash fresh morels under running water — they will waterlog and steam instead of sear. Use a dry pastry brush or soft toothbrush to brush away visible dirt. Halve each one lengthwise and inspect the hollow interior carefully for trapped grit or insects. If you find heavy grit, a very quick dunk in cold salted water followed by immediate drying on paper towels is acceptable — but they must be completely dry before the pan.

### 2. Salt your pasta water
Bring at least 5L of water to a full rolling boil. Season aggressively — roughly 10g salt per litre, until it tastes like the sea. Keep the lid on to maintain heat.

### 3. Sear the morels
Get your widest stainless or carbon steel skillet genuinely ripping hot over high heat before anything goes in. Add 30g of the butter and let it foam and subside almost immediately — the pan should be hot enough that it barely has time to brown. Add the morels in a single layer; work in two batches if needed — crowding is the most common mistake. Cook completely undisturbed for 2–3 minutes until deeply caramelised on one side. Toss and cook 1–2 minutes more. Season with salt and a little of the **1 g** white pepper. Transfer to a plate. Do not wipe the pan.

*Timer: 4 minutes*

### 4. Build the shallot base
Reduce heat to medium. Add 30g of butter to the same pan, letting it lift the fond. Once melted, add the shallots and cook gently, stirring occasionally, for 4–5 minutes until completely softened, sweet, and translucent — no colour at all.

*Timer: 5 minutes*

### 5. Deglaze and reduce the wine
Increase to high heat. Pour in the wine and scrape the pan immediately. Boil hard until almost completely evaporated and the shallots look glazed — about 2–3 minutes. All raw alcohol should be gone; trust your nose.

*Timer: 3 minutes*

### 6. Reduce the stock
Pour in the stock. Keep heat high and reduce by half — about 3–4 minutes. You should have roughly 100ml of intense, fragrant liquid remaining. This is doing the work that morel soaking liquid does in the dried-mushroom version.

*Timer: 3–4 minutes*

### 7. Cream reduction to nappe
Add the cream and tuck in the thyme sprigs. Bring to a lively simmer — not a rolling boil, which will break the fat. Reduce by one-third, stirring occasionally, for 6–8 minutes until the sauce coats the back of a spoon and a finger dragged through it leaves a clean line. Remove and discard the thyme. Return the seared morels to the sauce. Season with salt, the rest of the **1 g** white pepper, and the pinch of nutmeg. Hold at a gentle simmer.

*Timer: 7 minutes*

### 8. Cook the pasta
Drop the pappardelle into the boiling salted water. Fresh pasta cooks in 2–3 minutes — pull it just before al dente. Before draining, ladle out at least 250ml of pasta cooking water and set it aside.

### 9. Marry pasta and sauce
Using tongs, transfer the pasta directly from the pot into the sauce pan — bring all that clinging water with it. Set over medium heat. Add 45–60ml of pasta cooking water. Toss vigorously and continuously for 60–90 seconds until the sauce is glossy, cohesive, and clings to every strand. Add pasta water a splash at a time if it looks tight.

*Timer: 90 seconds*

### 10. Mount with butter and Parmesan
Pull the pan off the heat entirely. Add the remaining 20g of cold cubed butter first, tossing to melt it in — this is the gloss and body step. Then add the Parmigiano-Reggiano in two additions, tossing constantly between each. The residual heat does the work. If the sauce tightens, a small splash of pasta water loosens it.

### 11. Acid and plate
Squeeze in the juice of the ½ lemon and add a few gratings of zest. Toss once more — this is the step that makes the morel flavour lift and the richness feel clean rather than heavy. Taste and adjust salt.

Warm your bowls with hot water for 60 seconds, then dry them. Twirl generous portions with tongs into each bowl. Spoon any remaining morels and sauce over the top. Scatter the tarragon and finish with the olive oil. Serve within 90 seconds.

## Notes

**On searing temperature:** The pan must be genuinely hot before butter goes in. A warm or even medium-hot pan causes fresh morels to release their moisture and steam — you'll never recover the crust. If the butter foams for more than a few seconds, the pan wasn't hot enough.

**Batch searing:** If morels are large, sear in two batches without exception. One crowded batch of steamed mushrooms will flatten the entire dish.

**Stock quality:** You're reducing this by half, so whatever goes in gets amplified. Homemade or a good quality store-bought — not a bouillon cube.

**Madeira upgrade:** Swap white wine for Madeira or dry Marsala. It adds a nutty, oxidative depth that pairs extraordinarily well with fresh morels.

**The acid is non-negotiable:** Lemon at the finish is not garnish — it's structural. It cuts the cream, brightens the morel flavour, and is what separates a restaurant finish from a heavy sauce.

**Fresh pasta upgrade:** 250g 00 flour, 4 egg yolks + 1 whole egg, 5ml olive oil, pinch of salt. Knead 10 minutes, rest 30 minutes wrapped, roll to 2mm, cut wide pappardelle. The wider noodle cradles whole halved morels perfectly.

**Emulsification rescue:** If the sauce looks broken or greasy at the finish, add a splash of pasta water off the heat and toss hard — it re-binds immediately.
