# Hotteok Dutch Baby

**Serves 2** as a breakfast main, or **3–4** beside other dishes. One puffed custard pancake in a **25–26 cm cast-iron skillet** at *200 °C convection*, topped straight from the oven with a warm brown-butter *hotteok* filling—toasted nuts and seeds, dark brown sugar, cinnamon, and flaky salt—then finished at the table with fresh lemon or yuzu.

Companion file: `hotteok_dutch_baby_cook_live.html`

---

## Where it comes from

Built on Florence Fabricant's NYT Cooking Dutch Baby (Recipe #6648: 3 eggs, ½ cup flour, ½ cup milk, 1 tbsp sugar, nutmeg, 4 tbsp butter, 425 °F for 20 minutes then 300 °F for 5 minutes). The egg–flour–liquid ratio stays intact so the steam lift stays reliable.

Moved into a Korean *hotteok* register with three non-overlapping components: a properly salted, lightly spiced puffed custard; a crisp, sandy-molten brown-butter nut and cinnamon filling made on the stovetop while it bakes; and sharp fresh citrus at the table. Everything else stays out of the way.

---

## Ingredients

### Batter
- Large eggs, room temperature — **3** (~150 g)
- All-purpose flour — **65 g**
- Whole milk, room temperature — **120 g**
- Dark brown sugar (or muscovado / *heukseoltang*) — **10 g**
- Fine sea salt — **1.5 g**
- Ground cinnamon — **0.5 g**

### Skillet
- Unsalted butter — **30 g**

### Hotteok filling
- Walnuts or peanuts, coarsely chopped — **35 g**
- Sunflower seeds or pumpkin seeds — **10 g**
- White sesame seeds — **5 g**
- Unsalted butter — **20 g**
- Dark brown sugar — **30 g**
- Ground cinnamon — **2 g**
- Flaky sea salt — **0.5 g**

### Finish
- Lemon (or yuzu) — **1**, zest + **10–15 ml** fresh juice

---

## Method

### 1. Mix the batter
**Whisk** or **blend** *3* eggs, *65 g* flour, *120 g* milk, *10 g* dark brown sugar, *1.5 g* fine sea salt, and *0.5 g* ground cinnamon until completely smooth with no lumps.

If the eggs and milk came straight from the fridge, sit the whole eggs in warm tap water for *5 minutes* and microwave the milk for *15 seconds* until lukewarm (about *30 °C*, not hot) before mixing. Cold batter chills the skillet floor and cuts lift.

### 2. Rest the batter and preheat the skillet
**Leave** the batter on the counter for *15–20 minutes*. **Set** the empty *25–26 cm* cast-iron skillet on the middle rack and **heat** the oven to *200 °C convection* (`Convection Bake`) for at least *10 minutes*.

Resting hydrates the flour, relaxes the gluten, and lets blender foam subside so the edge bakes thin and crisp instead of bready. Preheat the skillet dry—butter left in a 200 °C oven scorches before you see it.

### 3. Melt the butter and pour
**Pull** the hot skillet onto a medium-low gas burner. **Swirl** in *30 g* unsalted butter until foaming and just starting to smell nutty (*20–30 seconds*), tilting to coat halfway up the walls. **Pour** the rested batter straight into the center and **slide** the skillet immediately onto the middle rack.

### 4. Bake
**Bake** at *200 °C convection* (`Convection Bake`) until the walls are tall and blistered and the rim is deep amber-gold. First look at *16 minutes*; do not open the oven door before *15 minutes*.

### 5. Toast the nuts and seeds
While the Dutch baby bakes, **toast** *35 g* chopped nuts, *10 g* sunflower or pumpkin seeds, and *5 g* white sesame seeds dry in a small skillet over medium heat, stirring often, until fragrant and lightly colored, *2–3 minutes*.

### 6. Build the hotteok filling
**Drop** *20 g* unsalted butter into the toasted nuts and **swirl** over medium heat until foaming and lightly browned, about *1 minute*. **Take** the pan off the heat, then **stir** in *30 g* dark brown sugar, *2 g* ground cinnamon, and *0.5 g* flaky sea salt. **Hold** warm off the burner until the pancake comes out.

Off the heat on purpose: residual heat softens the molasses into a glossy, sandy-molten *hotteok* filling without scorching the cinnamon or turning the sugar into hard caramel.

### 7. Read the Dutch baby
**Look** through the glass or open briefly at *16 minutes*.

> Tall blistered walls, deep amber-gold rim, puffed center with no wet glossy batter pooled in the middle: pull it now.
> Center still glossy and rim pale: close the door and bake *2–4 minutes* more, then check again.
> Rim going dark while the center is still wet: drop the oven to *160 °C* and give it *3 minutes*.

### 8. Fill, zest, and serve
**Spoon** the warm hotteok filling across the puffed center the moment the skillet leaves the oven. **Grate** the zest of *1* lemon (or yuzu) directly over top and **squeeze** *10–15 ml* fresh juice across the surface. **Cut** into wedges and serve immediately.

A Dutch baby drops fast once out of the oven; finish it in the skillet and bring it straight to the table while the rim is crisp.

## The call

Keep NYT 6648's 3-egg / 65 g flour / 120 g milk ratio on a preheated *25–26 cm* cast-iron skillet at *200 °C convection*, rest the batter *15–20 minutes*, and melt the skillet butter on the gas rather than blind in the oven. Keep the dark brown sugar, cinnamon, and nuts out of the 200 °C bake—toast them dry, brown *20 g* butter, stir the sugar and cinnamon in off-heat, and finish at the table with fresh lemon or yuzu.

## Red team

**Temperature and vessel.** NYT 6648 bakes at 425 °F (218 °C) conventional for 20 minutes, then drops to 300 °F (149 °C) for 5 minutes. Lowered ~20 °C for the Miele fan, that is *200 °C* (`Convection Bake`). Dropped the automatic 5-minute 150 °C finish: a tight Miele cavity barely cools over 5 minutes unless you vent the door, which collapses the puff. Bake straight at 200 °C and read the 16-minute visual gate, dropping to 160 °C only if the rim outruns the center. Calibrated for a *25–26 cm* cast-iron skillet; in a *30 cm* skillet the batter spreads thin and climbs less, so scale the batter to *4* eggs, *85 g* flour, *160 g* milk, *14 g* dark brown sugar, *2 g* fine salt, and *0.75 g* cinnamon.

**Why the hotteok filling stays out of the oven.** Scattering dark brown sugar onto 200 °C cast iron before pouring the batter burns the molasses bitter over 16–20 minutes, glues the bottom to the metal, and softens the nuts in the custard. Building the filling off-heat in a small pan takes 3 minutes while the pancake bakes, preserves full steam lift, and keeps the nuts crisp.

**Volatile flavors.** Cinnamon in the filling (*2 g*) goes in off-heat so it blooms without scorching. Lemon/yuzu zest and fresh juice go on at the table. Nuts and seeds toast dry first so their oils smell roasted rather than steamed.

**Fat.** NYT puts *57 g* (4 tbsp) butter straight into the skillet, which leaves the bottom greasy and gives the top nothing. Here *50 g* total butter is split into two non-overlapping jobs: *30 g* in the cast iron for clean release and crisp lace edges, and *20 g* browned with the toasted nuts for the filling.

**Salt, stacked.** NYT 6648 has *0 g* salt with unsalted butter.

| Source | Salt | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Batter (~347 g) | 1.5 g | Computed (~0.43% of batter) |
| Hotteok filling (~102 g) | 0.5 g | Flaky sea salt finish |
| **Total (~460 g plate)** | **2.0 g** | **~0.43% overall**, calibrated for a sweet butter-and-egg custard |

**Acid, stacked.** NYT 6648 has *0 ml* acid against 3 eggs and 57 g butter. Finishing with *10–15 ml* fresh lemon (or yuzu) juice plus the zest cuts *50 g* butter, 3 eggs, and *40 g* total dark brown sugar cleanly—classic German pancake acid meeting Korean *yuja*. Avoid *yuja-cheong* marmalade here; it stacks sugar on top of *30 g* brown sugar instead of cutting it.

**Technique gaps, closed.**
- Cold eggs/milk killing lift → warm-water bath and 15-second milk warm before blending.
- Unhydrated flour / trapped blender foam → *15–20 minute* batter rest while oven and skillet preheat.
- Scorched butter in a blind oven preheat → dry skillet preheat, butter swirled on medium-low gas.
- Collapsed rise → no door-opening before *15 minutes*.
- Hard-candy caramel or burnt cinnamon → sugar and cinnamon stirred into browned butter off-heat.

**Quantity.** Three eggs is a full breakfast main for **2**, not NYT's 3–4, unless other dishes are on the table.

**Computed.** Miele oven setpoint *200 °C* (`Convection Bake`), from 218 °C conventional minus ~20 °C. Batter salt *1.5 g* (~0.43%).

**Asserted, not cooked in this kitchen.** The *16–20 minute* bake window at 200 °C convection, the *30 g / 20 g* butter split, the *30 g* brown sugar and *50 g* nut/seed filling doses, and the *10–15 ml* citrus finish.
